Liar's Poker Launch Review

The rewrite works and survives bad connections. It is not yet a game strangers can pick up and enjoy. This page says where things stand, why the game still feels generated, and what has to happen before real players see it.

Game state

How a room moves

The server owns this flow and sends every player the full table after each change. The app only draws what it receives.

stateDiagram-v2
    [*] --> Lobby: create room
    Lobby --> Betting: host starts (2–8 seats)
    Betting --> Betting: bet higher (60 s per turn)
    Betting --> Reveal: check
    Betting --> Betting: timeout, server plays for you
    Betting --> Betting: player leaves, cards dealt again
    Reveal --> Betting: everyone ready, or 20 s
    Reveal --> Over: one player left
    Betting --> Over: everyone else left
    Over --> Betting: play again
    Over --> [*]: last person leaves

One deal: everyone gets as many cards as they have losses + 1. Bets claim a hand exists across all cards in play. A check reveals everything; whoever was wrong takes a card. A fourth card puts you out.

Why it feels generated

Engineered states, undesigned experience

Every case is handled, but nobody decided what a first-time player should understand in their first ten seconds. The UI exposes how the system is built instead of how the game feels. These are the specific reasons, worst first.

1

"Check" means the opposite of what poker players expect

In poker, check means "pass". Here it means "I think you're lying, show the cards". The most important button in the game is mislabelled for anyone who knows poker, and meaningless for anyone who doesn't.

Fix: call it "Call bluff" or "Challenge". Say what happens next on the button's first use: "All cards are shown. Whoever is wrong takes a card."

2

83 bets in 9 tabs, ranked unlike real poker

Straights rank below three of a kind, four of a kind below a flush, "Small poker" means straight flush. Players must learn a private ladder, and the picker never shows where a bet sits on it or how likely it is.

Fix: one scrolling ladder with the current bet marked and everything below it greyed out; a "Raise by one" shortcut; plain names ("Straight flush 9–K"); an optional odds hint while learning.

3

The number that matters is printed small

The whole game is reasoning about how many cards are in play and how many you hold. That sits in an 11 px subtitle ("5 cards in play"). The table centre is mostly empty space.

Fix: make the pool the centrepiece: "5 cards on the table · 2 are yours · 3 hidden", drawn as cards, updating every deal.

4

No teaching, only a wall of text

Rules live in a 16-section modal. It still says "2 to 10 players" (the limit is 8), while the hand panel says "out at 4" and the rules talk about holding 3. A new player is dropped straight into a live bet picker.

Fix: a guided first game against bots with three or four prompts at the moment they matter; fix the copy so limits match; keep the modal as reference only.

5

Placeholder screens that look real

The profile shows hard-coded Texas Hold'em statistics ("Flop 14%", "River 16%", "Time played 3.4h") that have nothing to do with this game. "Friends", "Add friends" and "Emotes" do nothing. This is the clearest sign of generated filler.

Fix: delete them now. Bring back only what the server records: games played, wins, average finishing place.

6

The reveal, the best moment, is a text summary

"1 of 2 on the table · Bot 3 takes a card" plus tiny cards inside small opponent cells. No pause, no flip, no clear "this card proves it". The 20-second auto-continue is not shown.

Fix: a short sequence: the claim, cards flip one hand at a time, matching cards light up and move to a row under the claim, then the verdict and the +1 card landing on the loser.

7

Silent and surprising on timeouts

No sound and no notification when it becomes your turn with the app in the background. If 60 seconds pass, the server checks for you, which can cost you a card without you understanding why.

Fix: a turn sound and push notification; a visible warning at 15 seconds; a timeline line that says "Time ran out, so you challenged automatically".

8

Generic look, system-side wording

Dark grey minimal theme, grey-on-grey text, a permanent 280 px sidebar on desktop holding four links. Copy like "Cards revealed", "Away · waiting", "Waiting for others (1)", and "deal" and "round" used for the same thing. The timeline mixes game moves with "lost connection / is back" noise.

Fix: one visual idea that belongs to this game (a table, cards with weight), higher contrast, one word per concept, connection events shown as a small marker instead of timeline rows.

9

Hard to get friends into a game

Rooms show a code, but there is no "Join with code" field and no invite link to share. Every room is public in the lobby list.

Fix: "Invite" button that shares a link and code, a code field in the lobby, private rooms by default.

Order of work

Suggested sequence

  1. Strip the fake parts. Remove placeholder profile stats, friends and emotes; rename Check; fix the rules copy. One small PR, biggest trust gain.
  2. Put the new server online next to the old one on a test address, with uptime checks and backups, and confirm an error report reaches PocketBase.
  3. Playtest with five people who have never played. Watch them without helping, write down every pause. This decides what step 4 actually contains.
  4. Design pass on the table: the card pool as centrepiece, the bet ladder, the reveal sequence, one visual identity. Then a guided first game.
  5. Invites and notifications: share link, join with code, private rooms, turn sound and push.
  6. Add the protocol version check, ship store builds, point the web build at the new server, merge the draft PR.
  7. Retire the old server once store traffic has moved over.

Code lives on python-server-rewrite (Firlej/liars-poker#1) and snapshot-client-rewrite (Aidem6/liars-poker-app#1, draft). Merging the app PR publishes the website, so it waits until step 6.
