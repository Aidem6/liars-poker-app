How to play

Never played? You'll have it after one round.

It's poker turned inside out. Nobody has a full hand. Instead, you bet on what everyone's cards make together.

Everyone gets one card

You see only your own. The deck is small: 24 cards, nines up to aces.

Bet on the whole table

A bet names a poker hand that's somewhere in all the cards together. A pair of queens means at least two queens between everyone.

Raise it, or call it a lie

On your turn, bet something higher. Or, if you don't believe the last bet, check: every card is turned over.

Two cards, taking a third. Four and you're out.
4

Whoever's wrong takes a card

The bet was there? The checker takes a card. It wasn't? The bettor does. At four cards you're out. The last player left wins.

The catch: more cards make bigger hands possible, so each card you lose helps you bet, and pushes you closer to the door.

Hands

Eleven hands, lowest to highest.

Every bet has to beat the one before it. The order is close to regular poker, with a few changes for the short deck.

  1. 1High cardAt least one of a rank
  2. 2PairTwo of a rank
  3. 3Two pairTwo different pairs
  4. 4Small straightNine to king, any suits
  5. 5Big straightTen to ace, any suits
  6. 6Three of a kindThree of a rank
  7. 7Full houseThree of one, two of another
  8. 8Four of a kindAll four of a rank
  9. 9FlushFive of one suit
  10. 10Small pokerSmall straight in one suit
  11. 11Big pokerBig straight in one suit

Within a hand, higher ranks win: a pair of tens beats a pair of nines, and a pair of aces beats them all. Two things differ from regular poker: straights rank below three of a kind, and a flush beats four of a kind.
